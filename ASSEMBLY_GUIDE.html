espWiimote — Hardware Assembly Guide

Step-by-step wiring for the ESP32-WROOM-32, the DFRobot IR Positioning Camera (SEN0158), a 4-channel bi-directional level shifter, and the GY-BNO08X gyro board.

Current board: esp32dev · I2C SDA=GPIO21 · SCL=GPIO22 · BNO RST=GPIO26
⚠️ Read first — do not skip the level shifter. The IR camera is treated as a 5 V device in this build. It must communicate through the level shifter. Never connect the camera's data lines (yellow/green) directly to the ESP32 or the BNO08X — 5 V on a 3.3 V pin can damage the ESP32 or the sensor. The BNO08X stays on the 3.3 V side at all times.

3. Pinout reference

3.1 ESP32-WROOM-32 DevKit

Only the pins used in this project are shown. The ESP32 is powered from USB (5 V).

On a 30-pin DevKit, GPIO21/GPIO22 are the standard I2C pins. This matches Wire.begin(21, 22) in include/app_config.h for the esp32dev env.

3.2 DFRobot IR Positioning Camera (SEN0158)

Red — VCC5 V (operating range 3.3–5 V)
Black — GNDground
Yellow — SDAI2C data (0x58)
Green — SCLI2C clock

Red and black connect to the HV (5 V) side of the shifter (power can be taken directly from the 5 V rail). Yellow/green (data) must pass through the shifter channels — they are the 5 V signals being converted down to 3.3 V.

3.3 4-channel bi-directional level shifter

The shifter has two reference rails and four data channels. Each channel (HVx ↔ LVx) converts voltages in both directions — perfect for I2C. We only use two channels (SDA and SCL).

HVhigh-voltage reference → 5 V
LVlow-voltage reference → 3.3 V
GNDground (both GND pins are common)
HV1 ↔ LV1channel 1 → SDA
HV2 ↔ LV2channel 2 → SCL
HV3/HV4, LV3/LV4unused — leave free

Naming: “HV” = high voltage side (5 V, camera side). “LV” = low voltage side (3.3 V, ESP32/BNO side). The middle number is the channel. Either GND pin works — they are the same net.

3.4 GY-BNO08X (pink board)

Pin order/labels can differ slightly between board revisions. Match by function, not by physical position. The common 8-pin layout is:

VCC / VIN3.3 V power
GNDground
SDAI2C data
SCLI2C clock
RSTreset → GPIO26
INTleave unconnected (not used)
PS0 / PS1 (mode)leave unconnected = I2C
ADR / AD0 (if present)leave unconnected = 0x4A (code probes both addresses)

The firmware probes both 0x4A and 0x4B, so the address pin position does not matter. PS0/PS1 select the protocol — leave them floating/low for I2C. The firmware uses reset-only init (RST on GPIO26, no INT), which is why RST is wired but INT is not.

4. Step-by-step assembly

  1. Unplug everything from power and USB. Assemble on a non-conductive surface. Never wire or re-wire while powered.
  2. Power rails first.
    • ESP32 3V3 → shifter LV (orange)
    • ESP32 VIN/5V → shifter HV (red)
    • ESP32 GND → shifter GND (black)
    • ESP32 GND → BNO08X GND (black)
    • ESP32 GND → camera black (black)

    Bring all grounds to a single common point.

  3. Power the boards.
    • ESP32 3V3 → BNO08X VCC/VIN (orange)
    • ESP32 VIN/5V → camera red (red)
  4. I2C data lines — 3.3 V side (BNO + shifter LV).
    • ESP32 GPIO21 → BNO08X SDA (yellow)
    • ESP32 GPIO22 → BNO08X SCL (green)
    • ESP32 GPIO21 → shifter LV1 (yellow)
    • ESP32 GPIO22 → shifter LV2 (green)
  5. I2C data lines — 5 V side (shifter HV → camera).
    • shifter HV1 → camera yellow (SDA) (yellow)
    • shifter HV2 → camera green (SCL) (green)
  6. BNO08X reset line.
    • ESP32 GPIO26 → BNO08X RST (blue)

    Leave INT, PS0/PS1, and ADR/AD0 unconnected.

  7. Double-check before powering. Verify: HV=5 V, LV=3.3 V, camera only on the HV side, BNO only on the 3.3 V side, common ground.

7. Power-on & verification

  1. Plug the USB cable in. The ESP32's power LED should light.
  2. Flash the firmware (pio run -t upload) and open the serial monitor at 115200 baud.
  3. Look for the startup lines:
    • IR camera found at 0x58 → camera detected.
    • BNO08X ready at 0x4A/0x4B (rotation vector enabled) → IMU detected.
  4. The I2C scan printed on boot should list the responding addresses (camera and BNO).
  5. Once on Wi-Fi, open the web page (URL printed in serial, or http://ircam.local) to see the live IR plot and the orientation box.
