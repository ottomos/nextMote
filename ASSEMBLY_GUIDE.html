nextMote — Hardware Assembly Guide

Step-by-step wiring for the ESP32-WROOM-32, the DFRobot IR Positioning Camera (SEN0158), and the GY-BNO08X gyro board. The level shifter is optional when the camera is powered from 3.3 V; it is required for 5 V camera wiring.

ESP32-WROOM · I2C SDA=GPIO21 · SCL=GPIO22 · BNO RST=GPIO26 · INT=GPIO27
⚠️ Power off before wiring. Do not put 5 V on nextMote GPIO. The SEN0158 works from 3.3–5 V. The tested wiring powers it from nextMote 3V3 and connects its I2C lines directly to GPIO21/22. If you instead power the camera from 5 V, route its SDA/SCL through the level shifter. Keep the BNO08X on the 3.3 V I2C side.

On a 30-pin DevKit, GPIO21/GPIO22 are the standard I2C pins. This matches shared sensor I2C bus used by the ESP-IDF camera and IMU drivers.

3.2 DFRobot IR Positioning Camera (SEN0158)

Red — VCC3.3 V tested; module range 3.3–5 V
Black — GNDground
Yellow — SDAI2C data (0x58)
Green — SCLI2C clock

Tested option: red VCC → nextMote 3V3, black GND → common ground, yellow SDA → GPIO21, and green SCL → GPIO22. No shifter is needed in this 3.3 V configuration. For the optional 5 V camera setup, use the shifter on both SDA and SCL; never put the camera's 5 V pull-ups directly on nextMote pins.

3.3 Optional 4-channel bi-directional level shifter (5 V camera option)

The shifter has two reference rails and four data channels. Each channel (HVx ↔ LVx) converts voltages in both directions — perfect for I2C. We only use two channels (SDA and SCL).

HVhigh-voltage reference → 5 V
LVlow-voltage reference → 3.3 V
GNDground (both GND pins are common)
HV1 ↔ LV1channel 1 → SDA
HV2 ↔ LV2channel 2 → SCL
HV3/HV4, LV3/LV4unused — leave free

Naming: “HV” = high voltage side (5 V, camera side). “LV” = low voltage side (3.3 V, nextMote/BNO side). The middle number is the channel. Either GND pin works — they are the same net.

3.4 GY-BNO08X (pink board)

Pin order/labels can differ slightly between board revisions. Match by function, not by physical position. The common 8-pin layout is:

VCC / VIN3.3 V power
GNDground
SDAI2C data
SCLI2C clock
RSTreset → GPIO26
INTactive-low data ready → GPIO27 (required)
PS0 / PS1 (mode)I2C mode; tie low if required by board revision
ADR / AD0 (if present)leave unconnected = 0x4A (code probes both addresses)

The firmware probes both 0x4A and 0x4B, so the address pin position does not matter. PS0/PS1 select the protocol — keep the board in I2C mode (low/default as marked for your revision). The ESP-IDF driver uses both RST on GPIO26 and the active-low INT on GPIO27.

4. Step-by-step assembly

  1. Unplug everything from power and USB. Assemble on a non-conductive surface. Never wire or re-wire while powered.
  2. Power rails first.
    • nextMote GND → BNO08X GND (black)
    • nextMote GND → camera black (black)
    • If using the optional 5 V camera setup: nextMote 3V3 → shifter LV and VIN/5V → shifter HV.

    Bring all grounds to a single common point.

  3. Power the boards.
    • nextMote 3V3 → BNO08X VCC/VIN (orange)
    • Tested: nextMote 3V3 → camera red (VCC) (orange).
    • Optional 5 V camera setup: nextMote VIN/5V → camera red (red). Do not also connect camera VCC to 3V3.
  4. Shared I2C data lines — 3.3 V side.
    • nextMote GPIO21 → BNO08X SDA and, for direct 3.3 V camera power, camera yellow SDA.
    • nextMote GPIO22 → BNO08X SCL and, for direct 3.3 V camera power, camera green SCL.
    • For the optional 5 V camera setup, connect GPIO21/GPIO22 to shifter LV1/LV2; camera SDA/SCL go to HV1/HV2 instead of directly to nextMote bus.
  5. BNO08X reset and data-ready lines.
    • nextMote GPIO26 → BNO08X RST (blue)
    • nextMote GPIO27 → BNO08X INT (purple, active-low data-ready)

    The ESP-IDF driver requires INT. Keep PS0/PS1 in I2C mode; tie both low if your board does not provide default pull-downs. Leave ADR/AD0 at the board default; firmware probes 0x4A and 0x4B.

  6. Double-check before powering. In the tested setup, camera and BNO use 3.3 V and share GPIO21/22. Only use the HV/LV shifter path when camera VCC is powered from 5 V.

7. Power-on & verification

  1. Plug the USB cable in. The nextMote's power LED should light.
  2. From firmware/wiimote-hid-test, build and flash the ESP-IDF project (idf.py build, then idf.py -p PORT -b 115200 flash). Replace PORT with nextMote serial port.
  3. Initialize Wi-Fi before checking sensors. If no network is saved, the board starts an open nextMote-Setup-XXXX network. Join it, stay connected despite any no-internet warning, and open http://192.168.4.1/ if the captive page does not open automatically. Choose a 2.4 GHz network and enter the password. Shared dual-band names work through their 2.4 GHz radio; ESP32-WROOM cannot join 5 GHz. On success, note the assigned IP, wait about 10 seconds for the restart, rejoin your home Wi-Fi and open that IP.
  4. Setup mode intentionally does not start Bluetooth, the camera or the IMU. Missing sensor startup messages in this mode are not a wiring failure. After successful setup, later boots join the stored network directly. Its DHCP address can change: read Wi-Fi connected; IP=... in the serial boot log. The fixed 192.168.4.1 address is for setup mode only.
  5. In normal mode, look for the startup lines:
    • SEN0158 initialized with DFRobot default profile → camera initialized.
    • BNO08x initialized at 0x4a or 0x4b → IMU found and rotation-vector reports enabled.
  6. With only the BNO connected, /api/imu should report detected:true; after the first rotation-vector event it should report available:true and roll, pitch, and yaw in degrees. On nxt12-imu-integration, also check accel_available:true, an increasing accel_samples count, and fresh accel_age_ms. On 6 November 2026, the user reported basic motion now seems to work in Mario Kart Wii through Dolphin + DolphinBar mode 4 with MotionPlus disabled, in addition to the earlier IMU-only web orientation check. This does not certify full gameplay or motion on real Wii/vWii.
  7. Open the web page at the IP printed in serial to see the live IR plot and the BNO08x orientation panel. The camera may report not detected during an IMU-only test; that does not prevent the IMU panel from working.
  8. Standard Wii Remote acceleration is implemented in reports 0x31/0x33 on the integration branch. An emulated MotionPlus from the BNO08x gyro is available only with the build flag -DNEXTMOTE_ENABLE_MOTION_PLUS=1 (off by default; not yet tested in a game). Check mounting axes/signs in wiimote-hid-test/main/wiimote_accel.h for the final controller mounting; default mapping is direct X/Y/Z. See the motion test checklist for remaining checks, including simultaneous camera/IMU hardware operation. A web preview or an open HID link alone is not proof that the console received motion data.

To reconfigure, press Wi-Fi setup in the normal web app or type setup in the 115200-baud serial console. This is one-shot and keeps the saved network; normal or a reset returns to normal mode when a network is saved. forget clears only Wi-Fi settings, not Bluetooth pairing. If the stored router is unavailable there is no automatic setup fallback: use serial setup. See the full initialization and recovery instructions.

Initialization validation, 7 November 2026: the user reports provisioning works really well. Laptop tests separately verified errors/retry, persistence and setup/reset triggers. The exact user browser and automatic captive-popup behavior were not specified.

Optional physical buttons - one Funduino shield

Physical and web buttons feed the same Wii Remote button state and HID report interface. The Mario Kart Wii test confirms physical input in the tested Dolphin setup; recording the platform does not imply a different button implementation for vWii.

The normal web app shows physical-input feedback prefixed PHY, for example PHY Pressed A and PHY Released A. This works without a Bluetooth connection and is separate from web-click feedback. Updates arrive about once per second; quick taps can show both transitions together. This checks device input, not whether a game received it.

Current temporary cabling plan, until custom hardware: use one Funduino joystick shield and set its 3.3 V/5 V lever to 3.3 V. The user confirmed the board layout: A=Up, C=Down, B=Right, D=Left, F=Wii A and E=Wii B. These assignments are for this shield prototype, not a finalized custom PCB.

The six-button prototype (merged from nxt16-physical-buttons) uses digital inputs only; joystick axes and click are unused. Physical input is off by default. Host debounce/source-merging tests and the enabled firmware build pass. The user wired F/E for Wii A/B, confirmed PHY A press/release and reported the physical input working in Mario Kart Wii in Dolphin. This confirms the physical-to-shared-Wii-button path, not all six controls. The user plans fuller button/game testing in a later task; the other buttons, combinations and vWii input remain unverified.

Power off before wiring. Use the shield's 3.3 V selector and supply, common ground, and verify 0-3.3 V button signals. Never connect 5 V signals to ESP32 GPIO. Use jumper wires, not direct Arduino-shield stacking. Leave analog axes, KEY and the 5 V rail unconnected.

Pin explanations and wiring restrictions

Pin / topicExplanation
Diagram colorsGreen: assigned signal. Blue: unused input candidate. Amber: reserved or boot-sensitive. Purple: power/ground.
UnusedUnassigned in the current sensor setup, not necessarily suitable for a button. No physical button functions have been assigned or enabled in firmware.
Numbered labelsSome boards add a D prefix: 15 and D15 refer to the same header pin.
17 / 16Also labeled TX2 / RX2. Unused UART2 pins can be used as ordinary digital inputs or outputs.
TR / TXThe reference's TR is the primary receive pin, normally labeled RX or RX0, opposite 34. TX may be labeled TX0. Keep both reserved for USB serial programming and logs.
22 / 21Shared camera + BNO I2C clock/data. The reference's outer GPIO23 callout at 22 is a typo; use 22 for SCL.
26 / 27BNO reset and active-low interrupt/data-ready, respectively.
VP, VN, 34, 35Input-only, without internal pull-ups. A button needs an external pull-up to 3v3.
32, 33, 25, 14, 13, 23, 19, 18, 17, 16, 4Unused input/output candidates with internal pull-ups. Reserve future LED, rumble, and speaker outputs before assigning all of them to buttons.
13 / 14Also JTAG pins; using JTAG would reserve these candidates.
5, 12, 2, 15Boot-strapping pins. Avoid for initial button wiring because switch states during reset can affect startup. Pin 2 may also drive an onboard LED.
EN / RESET / FLASHBoard enable/reset and onboard reset/boot controls are not spare gameplay inputs.
GND / 3v3 / VINBoth GND headers share common ground. Camera and BNO use 3v3 in the direct 3.3 V setup. VIN is not used for sensors in this setup; it is a board supply rail, not a logic input.
Button capacity15 unused non-strapping input candidates: 11 with internal pull-ups and four input-only pins needing external pull-ups. Enough for 11 gameplay buttons plus SYNC, subject to future output reservations.
WiringPower off before wiring. Buttons should switch their input to common GND with a suitable pull-up to 3v3. Never apply 5 V to an input.
