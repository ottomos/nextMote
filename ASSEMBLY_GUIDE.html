ESPmote — Hardware Assembly Guide

Step-by-step wiring for the ESP32-WROOM-32, the DFRobot IR Positioning Camera (SEN0158), and the GY-BNO08X gyro board. The level shifter is optional when the camera is powered from 3.3 V; it is required for 5 V camera wiring.

ESP32-WROOM · I2C SDA=GPIO21 · SCL=GPIO22 · BNO RST=GPIO26 · INT=GPIO27
⚠️ Power off before wiring. Do not put 5 V on ESP32 GPIO. The SEN0158 works from 3.3–5 V. The tested wiring powers it from ESP32 3V3 and connects its I2C lines directly to GPIO21/22. If you instead power the camera from 5 V, route its SDA/SCL through the level shifter. Keep the BNO08X on the 3.3 V I2C side.

On a 30-pin DevKit, GPIO21/GPIO22 are the standard I2C pins. This matches shared sensor I2C bus used by the ESP-IDF camera and IMU drivers.

3.2 DFRobot IR Positioning Camera (SEN0158)

Red — VCC3.3 V tested; module range 3.3–5 V
Black — GNDground
Yellow — SDAI2C data (0x58)
Green — SCLI2C clock

Tested option: red VCC → ESP32 3V3, black GND → common ground, yellow SDA → GPIO21, and green SCL → GPIO22. No shifter is needed in this 3.3 V configuration. For the optional 5 V camera setup, use the shifter on both SDA and SCL; never put the camera's 5 V pull-ups directly on ESP32 pins.

3.3 Optional 4-channel bi-directional level shifter (5 V camera option)

The shifter has two reference rails and four data channels. Each channel (HVx ↔ LVx) converts voltages in both directions — perfect for I2C. We only use two channels (SDA and SCL).

HVhigh-voltage reference → 5 V
LVlow-voltage reference → 3.3 V
GNDground (both GND pins are common)
HV1 ↔ LV1channel 1 → SDA
HV2 ↔ LV2channel 2 → SCL
HV3/HV4, LV3/LV4unused — leave free

Naming: “HV” = high voltage side (5 V, camera side). “LV” = low voltage side (3.3 V, ESP32/BNO side). The middle number is the channel. Either GND pin works — they are the same net.

3.4 GY-BNO08X (pink board)

Pin order/labels can differ slightly between board revisions. Match by function, not by physical position. The common 8-pin layout is:

VCC / VIN3.3 V power
GNDground
SDAI2C data
SCLI2C clock
RSTreset → GPIO26
INTactive-low data ready → GPIO27 (required)
PS0 / PS1 (mode)I2C mode; tie low if required by board revision
ADR / AD0 (if present)leave unconnected = 0x4A (code probes both addresses)

The firmware probes both 0x4A and 0x4B, so the address pin position does not matter. PS0/PS1 select the protocol — keep the board in I2C mode (low/default as marked for your revision). The ESP-IDF driver uses both RST on GPIO26 and the active-low INT on GPIO27.

4. Step-by-step assembly

  1. Unplug everything from power and USB. Assemble on a non-conductive surface. Never wire or re-wire while powered.
  2. Power rails first.
    • ESP32 GND → BNO08X GND (black)
    • ESP32 GND → camera black (black)
    • If using the optional 5 V camera setup: ESP32 3V3 → shifter LV and VIN/5V → shifter HV.

    Bring all grounds to a single common point.

  3. Power the boards.
    • ESP32 3V3 → BNO08X VCC/VIN (orange)
    • Tested: ESP32 3V3 → camera red (VCC) (orange).
    • Optional 5 V camera setup: ESP32 VIN/5V → camera red (red). Do not also connect camera VCC to 3V3.
  4. Shared I2C data lines — 3.3 V side.
    • ESP32 GPIO21 → BNO08X SDA and, for direct 3.3 V camera power, camera yellow SDA.
    • ESP32 GPIO22 → BNO08X SCL and, for direct 3.3 V camera power, camera green SCL.
    • For the optional 5 V camera setup, connect GPIO21/GPIO22 to shifter LV1/LV2; camera SDA/SCL go to HV1/HV2 instead of directly to the ESP32 bus.
  5. BNO08X reset and data-ready lines.
    • ESP32 GPIO26 → BNO08X RST (blue)
    • ESP32 GPIO27 → BNO08X INT (purple, active-low data-ready)

    The ESP-IDF driver requires INT. Keep PS0/PS1 in I2C mode; tie both low if your board does not provide default pull-downs. Leave ADR/AD0 at the board default; firmware probes 0x4A and 0x4B.

  6. Double-check before powering. In the tested setup, camera and BNO use 3.3 V and share GPIO21/22. Only use the HV/LV shifter path when camera VCC is powered from 5 V.

7. Power-on & verification

  1. Plug the USB cable in. The ESP32's power LED should light.
  2. From firmware/wiimote-hid-test, build and flash the ESP-IDF project (idf.py build, then idf.py -p PORT -b 115200 flash). Replace PORT with the ESP32 serial port.
  3. Look for the startup lines:
    • SEN0158 initialized with DFRobot default profile → camera initialized.
    • BNO08x initialized at 0x4a or 0x4b → IMU found and rotation-vector reports enabled.
  4. With only the BNO connected, /api/imu should report detected:true; after the first rotation-vector event it should report available:true and roll, pitch, and yaw in degrees.
  5. Open the web page at the IP printed in serial to see the live IR plot and the BNO08x orientation panel. The camera may report not detected during an IMU-only test; that does not prevent the IMU panel from working.
